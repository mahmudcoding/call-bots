# Call Bots 0.7.0

Google Meet works the way Aloqa does: paste a Meet link, choose how many bots,
send them. There is nothing to set up.

## Google Meet bots are guests now

**Google accounts are gone.** Meet bots used to need one signed-in Google
account each, managed in a panel behind the link field. They now join as
guests, exactly as Aloqa bots do — they type a name and the host admits them —
so there is no account to add, check, reconnect or run out of. The panel, the
mode switch and the `--as` flag are gone with it, and the saved sign-ins are
deleted the first time this version runs, since nothing could use or manage
them any more.

If your meetings were created by a personal Google account, note that Meet
turns anonymous visitors away from those. A bot says so on its card instead of
timing out. Meetings from a Google Workspace account take guests when the
admin allows it.

## What a Meet bot can do

Everything an Aloqa bot can, apart from choosing its send codec, which Meet
decides for itself:

- its own camera clip and voice, cycling through the five, one per bot
- **screen sharing**, new in this version — one bot at a time, because Meet
  removes the share control from everyone else while somebody presents
- microphone and camera state on arrival and from the card, per bot or fleetwide
- the stream monitor with per-stream codec, resolution, bitrate, loss and
  jitter, each stream named after the participant it belongs to
- the dark-camera watchdog, participant checks, live thumbnails
- add more bots to a running call, remove one, or stop everything

## Requirements, and what it does to your screen

Meet bots need **macOS** and **Google Chrome** installed. On the first send
the app builds a private copy of Chrome, which takes about half a minute and
as much disk as Chrome itself. macOS asks once for permission to control that
copy: allow it. It is the only permission Call Bots asks for — if anything
asks to record your screen, deny it.

The bots' windows stay hidden, like every other bot's browser. **Show windows**
on the Meet line brings them up when you want to watch one.

Expect about three Meet bots per 8-core Mac. Each is a full Chrome window
encoding and decoding video at the size a real participant's window has, so
what your call measures is what real people would cause.
